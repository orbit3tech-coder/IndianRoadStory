Indian RoadStory — Data and Privacy Policy

Effective 9 November 2026 · Orbit Technologies

Indian RoadStory, made by Orbit Technologies, does not collect your personal data on any server of ours: we have no accounts and no servers that receive it. Your location and recorded trips stay on your phone. The only exception is when the app asks OpenStreetMap for map tiles and road names, described below.

This policy covers the Indian RoadStory Android app, including earlier versions, and the Indian RoadStory web app. It takes effect on 9 November 2026.

Location

Indian RoadStory uses your precise location only while a tour is running, and only after you tap Start tour. It uses your position and direction of travel to work out which places are ahead, beside or behind you, and to show you on the live map.

  • Android app: location is read by a foreground service while a tour runs, with a notification showing for as long as it does. When you tap Stop tour, the app stops using location. It never asks for background ("all the time") location.
  • Web app: location comes from your browser, only while the page is open and a tour is running.
  • Route recording (on by default, can be turned off in Settings): your route is saved on your phone as a trip. See the next section.
  • Road names (on by default, can be turned off in Settings): while recording, the app sends a position about every 100 metres to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service, which replies with the road name. See Services the app connects to.

Your location is never sent to Orbit Technologies.

Your trips, settings and backups

Recorded trips hold the route points (latitude, longitude, time and GPS accuracy), the road names along the route, the trip's name and its distance. They are stored only on your phone: in the Android app's private storage, or in the web app's browser storage (IndexedDB) on that device. Your settings are stored the same way.

  • Android backup: if Backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may copy the app's data, including trips and settings, to your Google account under Google's terms. You can turn this off in Settings → Google → Backup.
  • Files you export: KML, GPX, CSV and backup files, and the Google Maps links you share or copy, contain your route. They go only where you choose to save or send them. Once you share them, the person or service you share them with controls them.
  • Restoring a backup: the app reads the file you pick and adds its trips on your phone. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Web app: clearing your browser's data for the site deletes your trips and settings. Use Back up all trips if you want to keep them.

Services the app connects to

These services are run by other organisations under their own privacy policies. When the app contacts them, they receive your IP address and basic device information, as any website does.

Service When What it receives
OpenStreetMap tile servers (OpenStreetMap Foundation) When the map is on screen The map tiles requested, which show the area you are viewing, and your IP address
OpenStreetMap Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation) While recording a route with road-name lookup on, about once every 100 m and at most once a second A position (latitude and longitude) and the app's name and version
Google Maps (Google) Only when you tap a Google Maps link for a trip The route points in that link
Your phone's or browser's text-to-speech engine When an announcement has no packaged recording The announcement text, which is a landmark description and contains no personal data. Some browser voices are processed online by the browser's maker
Google Play (Android) When you install or update the app Install and crash information Google Play collects under Google's terms
Web hosting provider (web app) When you open the web app Standard server logs, such as IP address, browser type and pages requested

The OpenStreetMap Foundation says it logs IP addresses and may analyse Nominatim queries to improve the map; see its privacy policy. To stop sending positions to Nominatim, turn off Look up road names in Settings.

You can withdraw any permission at any time in your phone's Settings → Apps → Indian RoadStory → Permissions. The app then stops the features that need it.

What we don't do

  • No accounts or sign-in, and we don't ask for your name, email or phone number.
  • No advertising, and no advertising or tracking IDs.
  • No analytics, tracking or crash-reporting tools of our own.
  • No access to your contacts, photos, microphone or camera.
  • We don't sell, rent or share your data, because we never receive it.

Keeping and deleting your data

Everything the app stores stays on your phone until you delete it, and you can delete it at any time.

  • One trip: open it in My trips and tap Delete trip.
  • Everything in the Android app: Settings → Apps → Indian RoadStory → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. Copies in your Google account backup follow Google's backup settings.
  • Everything in the web app: clear your browser's site data for Indian RoadStory.
  • Files you exported stay wherever you saved or sent them until you delete them there.

Children

Indian RoadStory is a travel guide for general audiences and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, including children under 18. Location and trips stay on the device and are never sent to us.

Security

Trips and settings are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read, or in the browser storage of the web app's own site. All connections to OpenStreetMap and the web app use encrypted HTTPS. Anyone who can unlock your phone can see your trips, so keep a screen lock on.

Your rights

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you have the right to know what personal data is processed about you, to have it corrected or erased, to nominate someone to act for you, and to have grievances addressed. Because your location and trips never leave your phone except as described above, you can see, correct and delete all of it yourself in the app.

  • Turn features off at any time: route recording and road-name lookup in Settings, location in your phone's or browser's settings.
  • Data held by OpenStreetMap: requests about tile or Nominatim logs go to the OpenStreetMap Foundation, under its privacy policy.
  • Anything else: contact us using the details below. You may also complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts handling data differently, we will update this policy, change the date at the top, and point out important changes in the app's About screen before they take effect.
